## Goal

Use TikTok to reach new listeners with short-form music-friendly content.

## Why this matters

TikTok can surface music quickly through trends, sounds, and discoverability.

## Prerequisites

- TikTok account
- Short-form content ideas
- Audio or visual hooks

## Accounts & tools

- TikTok profile
- Mobile recording tools
- Editing apps

## Estimated cost

- FREE to start.
- PAID for editing tools or music licensing.

## Time required

- 4–6 hours to plan and post the first videos.

## Step-by-step

### Step 1 — define your TikTok identity

Choose a tone:
- playful
- emotional
- cinematic
- behind-the-scenes

### Step 2 — create native hooks

Use strong first 1–2 seconds:
- a compelling line of lyrics
- a visual surprise
- a production moment

### Step 3 — plan content types

- Music teaser clips
- Studio process
- Reaction or story posts
- Trend-based videos using your audio

### Step 4 — post consistently

- 2–4 videos per week.
- Reuse audio and themes.
- Respond to comments and duets.

### Step 5 — measure early signals

Track:
- Views
- Likes
- Shares
- Follows
- Sound clicks

## What you should see

- A TikTok content plan.
- 2–3 posted videos.
- Early performance notes.

## What to record

- Hook ideas.
- Post concepts.
- Engagement results.

## Metrics

- Views
- Engagement rate
- Followers
- Sound usage

## Common errors

- Posting non-native content.
- Ignoring trend formats.
- Using weak hooks.

## Troubleshooting

- If a video performs poorly, test a new hook.
- If you lack ideas, adapt a trend to your music.
- If you post too rarely, focus on consistency.

## Notes

- TikTok is about experimentation and fast feedback.
- Keep the content authentic and music-focused.
