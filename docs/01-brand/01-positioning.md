# Phase 1 — Artist Positioning

## Goal

Write a strong foundational positioning statement for `ajkay`.

## Why this matters

A positioning statement helps every later decision: content, ads, outreach, and artist story.
Without it, your marketing feels scattered and less memorable.

## Step-by-step

### Step 1 — answer the core questions

Write short answers to each:
- Who is `ajkay`?
- What type of music do you make?
- What feelings or moments does your music create?
- Who do you want your music to reach?

### Step 2 — build your artist statement

Use this formula:

`[Artist name] makes [music style] for [listener type] who want [emotional outcome].`

Example:

`ajkay makes intimate electronic pop for listeners who want cinematic late-night moods and emotional clarity.`

### Step 3 — expand to a brand profile

Write 3 sections:
- Sound: a 1–2 sentence description of your music.
- Story: a 1–2 sentence reason why you create.
- Experience: what listeners feel or do when they hear your music.

### Step 4 — pick reference artists

Choose 3–5 artists whose audience, mood, or style feels closest to your sound.
For each, note what matches:
- Tone / energy
- Visual style
- Audience vibe

## What to record

- The positioning sentence.
- Sound, story, and experience bullets.
- Reference artist list and reasons.

## Common mistakes

- Using only genre labels.
- Writing a statement that is too broad.
- Choosing reference artists without a clear reason.

## Notes

- This is a working draft. You can refine it later.
- Use the same language in your bio, pitch copy, and ad headlines.
